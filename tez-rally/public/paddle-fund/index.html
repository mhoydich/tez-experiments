the paddle fund · a playable concept · no money moves here

A new paddle every week.

Ten players put in twenty dollars a week. That is exactly one $200 paddle, so one lands every week for ten weeks, and the ladder decides who unboxes first. When your week comes up you take it, punt it to a bigger kit, or pass it on.

the ledger · public, in order

    01 · THE SPRINT

    Ten weeks on, sixteen off

    Twenty dollars a week for ten weeks, twice a year. Four hundred dollars a year and you never play a dead paddle again. Between sprints there are no dues at all.

    02 · THE ORDER

    Play sets it. Never a draw.

    Your standing on the countersigned ladder at kickoff is your slot. The club picks the direction: champions first, or bottom of the ladder first as a handicap.

    03 · THE CALL

    Take. Punt. Pass.

    Take the paddle you pick, up to $200. Punt and your $200 waits for you — next sprint you are holding $400, two punts max. Pass it to a new player or the loaner bag.

    The guardrails

    • Rotation, choice, or skill — never chance. Paid entry plus a random winner is a raffle, and raffles are regulated. Nobody wins this. Everybody gets their turn.
    • Dollars in equal dollars out. Miss weeks and your take is pro-rata. Leave and your balance leaves with you.
    • New seats go late. The circle's one real risk is someone unboxing in week one and ghosting. First sprint, you unbox in the back half.
    • The side pot is the only prize, and it is earned. Order as a team, bank the discount, and pay it out for the ladder, for improvement, for showing up — or punt it toward a ball machine.
    • The ledger is public. Every week is one line anyone can read.

    Mostly already built.

    This page is a rehearsal. The pieces a real sprint needs are standing around the yard: a circle contract that already pays a pot in rotation, a ladder that only moves when every player signs the score, and receipts that say what the money bought.
