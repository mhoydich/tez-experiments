Before the year

Jan 16, 2025rule

UPA-A publishes its 2025 equipment rules

The pro tours' certifying body announced its 2025 Equipment Rules and Regulations, with registration opening Feb 1 and compliance required for all professional play by Sep 1, 2025. The standards were developed with Pickle Pro Labs and UMass-Lowell engineers, and amateur events stayed on USA Pickleball's list. pickleball.com ↗

Jan 20, 2025money

World No. 1 Federico Staksrud signs with adidas

adidas Pickleball announced a multi-year partnership with Federico Staksrud, with a signature paddle planned for April 2025. thedinkpickleball.com ↗

Jan 31, 2025rule

USAP media day: more enforcement, Ronbus Ripple fails PBCoR

At its Jan 31 media day USA Pickleball said production Ronbus Ripple paddles measured above 0.44 PBCoR after pre-production samples had passed, acknowledged the Chorus Fire was delisted and relisted within days, and promised expanded field testing. A spin-rate test was planned for Q2 2025. speakpickleball.com ↗

Apr 1, 2025rule

UPA-A removes every Luzz paddle from its approved list

After a Pickleball Tech Dude video questioned the Luzz Cannon, UPA-A said Luzz had submitted it as a design variation of the provisionally approved ZZ model and pulled the whole brand. UPA-A president Jason Aspes: 'Violating UPA-A rules has consequences.' Luzz models returned to the list in the fall. thedinkpickleball.com ↗

Apr 2, 2025money

US tariffs on Chinese goods hit paddle makers

Tariffs announced Apr 2 raised import costs on China-made paddles by as much as 145 percent, according to CRBN founder Garrett Gosselin, who called the impact on his family-run company 'very real' and did not rule out price increases. CRBN, JOOLA and Selkirk all manufacture in mainland China. worldpickleballmagazine.com ↗

Apr 14, 2025rule

UPA-A adds destructive 'artificial break-in' testing

UPA-A and Pickle Pro Labs introduced the Artificial Break-In standard, which compression-cycles a paddle to simulate months of play. From Sep 1, 2025 paddles must test at or under 0.385 PEF when new and may not exceed 0.405 PEF after break-in. thedinkpickleball.com ↗

Apr 18, 2025legal

USA Pickleball countersues JOOLA

Answering JOOLA's 2024 lawsuit, USAP filed a counterclaim against parent Sport Squad alleging JOOLA submitted toned-down Gen 3 paddles for approval and then sold versions with as much as double the foam under USAP's approved mark. pickleball.com ↗

Jul 1, 2025rule

USAP's first PBCoR sunset list takes effect

Paddles that exceeded the PBCoR standard were removed from sanctioned tournament play: JOOLA Perseus 14mm and 16mm Mod TA-15, Gearbox Pro Power Elongated, and ProKennex's 11mm Black Ace Ovation, Pro, LG and XF. USAP reversed the Black Ace LG's delisting on Jun 3 after new testing. usapickleball.org ↗

Aug 19, 2025legal

Court gives preliminary approval to JOOLA's $790,000 Gen 3 class settlement

Sport Squad (JOOLA) agreed to pay $790,000 to settle class actions alleging Gen 3 paddles were misrepresented as USAP approved, denying wrongdoing. Buyers returning a paddle could claim a $300 refund with proof of purchase or a $150 JOOLA.com code without; claims closed Dec 15, 2025. classaction.org ↗

Sep 1, 2025rule

UPA-A certification becomes mandatory for pro play

From Sep 1, 2025 only UPA-A certified paddles, tested to the 0.385 new / 0.405 post-break-in PEF limits, could be used in PPA Tour and MLP professional events. thedinkpickleball.com ↗

Oct 23, 2025rule

Pro paddles keep failing on-site tests; UPA-A sets fines

Nearly two months into the new standard, UPA-A confirmed paddles from six manufacturers had failed on-site testing and published a penalty schedule: $1,000 for a first offense in 2025, up to $10,000 for repeats, and up to $50,000 with possible decertification from 2026. A quarterly retail-purchase compliance protocol starts January 2026. thedinkpickleball.com ↗

Nov 1, 2025rule

USAP power ceiling drops to PBCoR .43

The limit was .44 from Nov 1, 2024 and tightens to .43. Every 2026 paddle is designed under this ceiling, which is why the year's story is spin and feel, not raw power. equipment.usapickleball.org ↗

Dec 11, 2025money

USA Pickleball partners with Pickleball Instruments for field testing

USAP announced a long-term partnership to deploy Pickleball Instruments' testing technology at amateur tournaments from January 2026, to verify paddles meet approved standards and to flag counterfeits. usapickleball.org ↗

Dec 17, 2025legal

CRBN sues Vatic Pro over its full-foam core patent

US Patent 12,465,827. Reported as quietly settled about a week before JOOLA's filing in April. thedinkpickleball.com ↗

Dec 31, 2025money

Anna Leigh Waters' Paddletek contract expires

The world No. 1's agreement with Paddletek ended on Dec 31, 2025, opening the most valuable paddle free agency the sport had seen; her next deal was reported within days in January 2026. thedinkpickleball.com ↗

Oct – Nov 2026rests on a rule

A certification-rush launch cluster

Anything submitted before Oct 1 is certified for life under the old roughness rule. Brands with gritty faces had every reason to submit early and launch later. Expect a run of releases through Nationals week whose certification dates read August and September.

Oct – Dec 2026rests on a rule

The tour-only grit brands come to USAP

Six Zero's Coral Pro, both 11SIX24 lines and Honolulu's Crystal Blue are UPA-A only: their faces pass the tour's spin-output test and do not pass USAP's surface-roughness test. With USAP adopting the same 2,100 RPM output test, the reason to stay off the USAP list gets weaker. Look for USAP versions.

2027inference

$99 becomes the real floor, and $300 has to explain itself

Full foam with durable grit is now sold at $99 to $120 by Vatic Pro, Ronbus, Enhance and Spartus. The top tier is answering with things a $99 paddle cannot copy quickly: removable weight systems, signature shapes, co-brands. Expect more tuning hardware and fewer plain $280 paddles.

2027inference

Fewer brands

Patent royalties, a $10,000 annual UPA-A brand fee plus $3,000 per paddle, a new spin test, and two funded acquirers. The long tail of rebadged-catalog brands gets squeezed from every side.

2027open question

The unresolved rule: break-in

UPA-A certifies for two years and tests after artificial break-in. USAP certifies for life and tests fresh. USAP's own field testing found 6% of paddles failing, mostly worn-in ones. The next big rule fight is whether USAP adds a post-wear standard.

Ten trends, read off the calendar.

01

Foam won

Of the releases on this calendar, the large majority are full-foam Gen 4 builds. John Kew called roughly 90% foam share by year-end back in January and said in June that more than half the pros had switched. The last holdouts moved this year: Paddletek in January, Wilson and HEAD by summer, JOOLA on Sep 30. The exceptions are deliberate: JOOLA's Pro V and Proton's Coyote stayed Gen 3, Gearbox builds on carbon ribs, Owl damps a honeycomb for quiet. johnkewpickleball.beehiiv.com ↗

02

The year of durable grit

With power capped at PBCoR .43, the fight moved to spin that lasts. Nearly every challenger launched a named surface: HexGrit, EverGrit, NanoBond, PermaGrit, Crystal Blue, Harmony Grit, SilverBack, Diamond Tough, SharkSkin. Pickleball Effect wore them down in the lab and measured what was left. John Kew reported that 76% of spin loss lands in the first 50 hits. pickleballeffect.com ↗

03

EPP for touch, MPP for pop

Foam stopped being one thing. EPP is the softer, more muted bead foam; MPP is firmer and livelier. Enhance sells the same paddle both ways, Gearbox named its two Pressure X models after the letters, and cores now stack three or four densities with EVA or TPU rings to push twistweight up. Player surveys say the foam outlasts the face: grit wear first, then face disbonding, then edge guards. thedinkpickleball.com ↗

04

Two rulebooks, one number

USAP governs sanctioned amateur play and certifies for life on a fresh paddle. UPA-A governs the pro tours, certifies for two years, and tests after artificial break-in. Brands now ship to one list or the other on purpose: the Coral Pro, both 11SIX24 lines and Honolulu's Crystal Blue are tour-only, their grit too rough for USAP's surface test, the TKO-X 14mm failed USAP's power test and sells as UPA-A only, Selkirk makes a weightless OMNI just for pros. On Oct 1 USAP adopts the tour's 2,100 RPM spin cap, the first real convergence. thedinkpickleball.com ↗

05

Patents became a design constraint

CRBN enforced a full-foam patent against Vatic Pro. JOOLA took eleven brands to the ITC over the floating core and the throat foam block, and eight have settled for royalties, patent marking and phase-outs. Three of the year's launches (Reserve Honeyfoam, TKO-X, and Proton's pre-Coyote line) already have end dates because of it. pickleball.com ↗

06

The barbell

The top of the market held: Boomstik $333, OMNI $300, Pro V $299.95, Barrage and Pressure X $280. The bottom collapsed upward in quality: full foam with durable grit at $99 to $120. The middle is where the best reviews landed (Holbrook Fuze $230, Honolulu CR $195, Power 2 $210), and it is where JOOLA chose to put its first foam paddle. Amazon's 2025 paddle revenue grew 55% on 38% more units, so buyers are still trading up. thedinkpickleball.com ↗

07

Tune it yourself

Lead tape went factory. Selkirk's OMNI ships with removable 7.5 g perimeter weights, the SLK Geo and Dauntless carry the same MOI system at $100 and $180, Luzz has side wings, Friday built a weighting ring into the rim. Reviewers now publish swingweight and twistweight for every paddle, and brands are designing to those numbers. pickleballeffect.com ↗

08

One platform, every shape

The release is a family now: three or four shapes, often two thicknesses, one core. The hybrid is the shape of the year, to the point that JOOLA added one (Kosmos) and built Staksrud's and McGuffin's paddles on it. Widebodies are getting flagship builds instead of the hand-me-down. pickletip.com ↗

09

Money arrived, and it is buying

Selkirk took $30M and bought Bread & Butter. Paddletek Group, backed by Thirty-5 Capital, bought PIKKL. Pickleball Inc. raised $225M and owns both pro tours and the largest paddle retailer. The sport had 24.3 million US players in 2025, up 22.8%. The equipment business is starting to look like golf's. thekitchenpickle.com ↗

10

Quiet is a category

USAP created a Quiet Category (under 80 dB, under 600 Hz) and Owl is the first brand in it with a paddle that also passes for tournament play. Selkirk's investors list a quiet line in the plan. For anyone whose courts sit near houses, this is the release that matters most. usapickleball.org ↗

How to read this.

  1. Every entry links to where it came from: brand press releases, the approval lists, and the reviewers who measure things (Pickleball Effect, John Kew, Pickleheads, The Kitchen, The Dink, Pickleball Studio, Pickleball Blast, PickleTip).
  2. Dates carry a precision. 'Day' means a press release or launch article names it. 'Month' means reviews and listings bracket it. 'Quarter' means we know roughly when and say so.
  3. Confidence is marked on each line. High: a first-party page plus a reviewer, or two independent sources. Medium: one good source. Low: a retailer listing or an approval-list entry only.
  4. Certification is shown only where a source states it. Blank does not mean unapproved. Check the USAP and UPA-A lists before a tournament.
  5. Forecasts are labeled by what they rest on: a rule, a pattern, an inference, or an open question. None of them is inside information.
  6. Prices are US list prices at launch. Most direct-to-consumer brands run a standing 10% reviewer code.
