01 / Build your game

A little better.

You don’t need a perfect game to belong on court. Pick a starting point, practice with purpose, and move forward when the checkpoint feels repeatable.

Get into the game

Learn the rules, start a rally, and finish your first doubles game with confidence.

Start here if Choose this if serving, the kitchen, or the score still makes you pause.

01

1. Build one reliable serve

Pick a legal serve motion and a generous diagonal target. Repeat the same setup.

Try it Hit 10 serves from each side at a towel-sized target safely inside the service court. Start with a drop serve if the bounce makes timing easier.

Your checkpoint Can you land 8 of 10 legally from each side without rushing? Increase placement before pace.

02

2. Start every rally correctly

Return the serve after its bounce. Stay ready for the return bounce when your team serves.

Try it With a partner, play 10 serve–return–third-shot sequences. Say 'bounce' on the first two contacts, then continue cooperatively.

Your checkpoint Complete 10 starts with no early volley. Then aim the return deeper and recover your balance.

03

3. Know when to step in

A dink is a soft bounced shot near the net. Enter for the bounce; get fully out before a volley.

Try it Trade 10 soft dinks, including a short feed that invites you into the kitchen. Recover outside it, then receive one gentle volley feed and finish balanced.

Your checkpoint Can you explain why a bounced shot inside is legal and a volley with kitchen contact is a fault?

04

4. Call a doubles game

Agree on side-out scoring. Say all three numbers before each serve.

Try it Walk through 0–0–2, an opening side out, a point won by the next team, then first-server and second-server faults. Play a game with a partner checking each call.

Your checkpoint You can identify the serving team, correct side, and server number after both a point and a lost rally.

Build a dependable doubles game

Use depth, soft hands, and shared court coverage to earn a better next ball.

Start here if Choose this if you know the rules and can sustain a rally, but drops, resets, or partner positioning vary under pressure.

01

1. Give the forehand a shape

Turn early, meet the ball in front, and finish toward the target. Make clean placement repeatable.

Try it Ask a partner for 10 comfortable forehand feeds. Send five crosscourt and five through the middle, then alternate the targets on their call.

Your checkpoint At least 7 of 10 reach the called target area in court without changing your swing into a hard slap.

02

2. Drop, observe, then advance

Give the ball enough arc to land softly in the opposing kitchen. Move only as far as your shot earns.

Try it Start a few steps behind the kitchen line. Hit five drops, step back after a successful set, and work toward the baseline. Stop and prepare as your partner contacts the ball.

Your checkpoint From each distance, land 3 of 5 soft drops that your partner plays from below net height. A miss is useful feedback, not a reason to rush in.

03

3. Reset a difficult ball

A reset takes pace off an incoming ball and sends it softly toward the kitchen. Shorten the stroke.

Try it Stand in midcourt while a partner feeds controlled volleys. Reset five to the middle kitchen, then take one step forward after a good reset and stop for the next feed.

Your checkpoint Reset 3 of 5 feeds without a high attackable bounce. Repeat at the same pace before making the feed harder.

04

4. Own the right side together

Facing the net, call the seam before play and move with your partner. Court side is a role, not a permanent assignment.

Try it Begin with one player on the right. Opponents feed toward the right sideline and middle in turn. Call 'mine' or 'yours' early, recover together, then swap roles.

Your checkpoint Complete 10 feeds with no collision, silent middle ball, or two-player chase. For a left-handed player, agree coverage again rather than assuming the same forehand geometry.

Turn control into pressure

Train decisions under variation: when to attack, when to reset, and how to cover the next ball.

Start here if Choose this if your fundamentals hold in games and you want to test decisions, disguise, and doubles patterns under pressure.

01

1. Create a weaker next contact

Change dink placement while keeping an unattackable height. Read the reply instead of forcing a winner.

Try it Trade four neutral crosscourt dinks, then move the fifth toward a different kitchen target. Resume the rally and notice whether the opponent must reach or moves comfortably.

Your checkpoint Across 10 pattern changes, keep at least 7 in and recover before the next contact. Track pop-ups as well as outright misses.

02

2. Choose drop or drive on purpose

Use your contact height, balance, and opponents' positions to choose. Prepare for the fifth shot either way.

Try it A feeder alternates deep returns and shorter sits-up balls. Call 'drop' or 'drive' before your third contact, play the next two shots, and explain the choice afterward.

Your checkpoint Review 10 sequences: was the choice playable, and did you recover for the reply? Count the quality of the fifth shot, not just the third.

03

3. Attack a ball you can support

A speed-up changes a soft exchange into a faster one. Attack from balance and prepare for a counter.

Try it After three cooperative dinks, the feeder varies a low ball and a higher ball. Reset the low ball; on a suitable higher contact, speed up at controlled pace and play the reply.

Your checkpoint Make 8 of 10 sensible decisions without a kitchen fault. A kept-in reset earns as much credit as a controlled attack.

04

4. Cover the counter as a pair

Before the ball speeds up, agree who covers the seam and who protects the line. Recover after every contact.

Try it In four-player practice, start at the kitchen line. The right-side player changes one ball's pace, the partner covers the agreed middle, and both play two more shots. Swap right-side roles after five starts.

Your checkpoint Across 10 starts, both players are ready for the next shot and can describe the same coverage plan. Add randomness only when the pair stays coordinated.

Before the first serve.

Start behind the baseline

At serve contact, keep at least one foot on the ground behind the baseline. Neither foot may touch the baseline, court, or ground outside the extended sideline and centerline.

Volley serve or drop serve

Volley serve: hit before a bounce, swinging upward; contact at or below waist level with the paddle head no higher than the wrist. Drop serve: release from a natural, unaided height without propelling the ball, let it bounce, then hit. The three volley-serve motion restrictions do not apply to the drop serve. Keep the release visible and do not add spin on release.

Diagonal, beyond the kitchen

Serve into the opposite diagonal service court. Its kitchen line is out on a serve; the other service-court lines are in. A net-touching serve that lands legally remains in play.

One bounce on each side first

Let the serve bounce before returning it. The serving team must then let that return bounce. After these two bounces, volleys are allowed outside the kitchen.

The kitchen is for bounced balls

You may enter the kitchen to play a bounced ball. Before volleying again, place both feet fully outside it. During a volley, you, your gear, and anything touching you must avoid the kitchen and its lines. Momentum into it, or something touching it, is a fault even after the rally ends.

Call three numbers in doubles

Only the serving team scores. Call serving score, receiving score, then server 1 or 2. Begin 0–0–2: the opening team gets one server. Later, both partners serve before side out. Usually play to 11, win by two. Confirm the format; rally scoring works differently.

  3. 5min

    Two dink targets

  4. 3min

    Score rehearsal

    Walk through an opening 0–0–2 side out, then both servers on the other team. Each player calls the next score and points to the correct server. Finish by naming one shot to take into the next game.

    Touch and rhythm

  2. 6min

    Forehand targets

    Run crosscourt-to-middle forehands, three minutes in each role. Keep a count of called targets reached.

  3. 8min

    Drop ladder

    Work away from the kitchen line toward the baseline. Move back after a successful set; trade roles halfway.

  4. 8min

    Reset and advance

  5. 5min

    Apply and review

    Play unscored full-court practice rallies beginning with a diagonal serve, the return bounce, and a chosen drop or drive. Switch ends halfway. Name one repeatable cue and one thing to revisit.

45 minutes · doubles decisions

Prepare together, protect the seam, and support every attack with a next-shot plan.

  1. 5min

    Pair warm-up

    Begin with cooperative dinks and controlled groundstrokes. Agree who covers the middle with today's handedness and court sides.

  2. 8min

    Four-player opening patterns

    Run serve–return–third-shot starts with all four players, then play one more ball. Rotate the server every two minutes and count balanced next contacts.

    Dink placement

    Play, then name the pattern

The drill lab.

Simple setups. Clear targets. Open a drill and get going.

01Serve to a generous targetbeginner · 6 min · 1–2+

Set up Use a court, 5–10 balls, and a flat target marker well inside each diagonal service court. Keep markers away from anyone's footing.

  1. Choose a volley serve or drop serve and check the corresponding rules above.
  2. Hit 10 from the right, then 10 from the left; collect balls between sets.
  3. Count legal serves separately from serves that reach the target. Repeat with the same routine.

Build it After 8 of 10 legal serves from each side, make the target smaller or deeper. Change one variable at a time.

Success target 8 of 10 legal serves from each side; 5 of 10 in the chosen target area.

02Return deep, arrive readybeginner · 6 min · 2+

Set up One player serves; the other receives behind the baseline. Choose a large return target several feet inside the opposite baseline.

  1. Let the serve bounce, return into the deep target, and begin moving toward the kitchen line.
  2. The server lets the return bounce before hitting a controlled third shot. The receiver stops and gets ready as that player contacts the ball.
  3. Play one more shot, reset, and repeat five times before swapping roles.

Build it Vary the serve's placement. If the return is weak, practice stopping earlier and handling the next shot before advancing again.

Success target 7 of 10 returns in court, with no two-bounce-rule violation and a balanced next contact.

USA Pickleball · Rules Summary ↗
03Four-target dink controlbeginner · 8 min · 2+

Set up Stand diagonally opposite near the kitchen lines. Choose two generous landing areas in each kitchen; point to them instead of placing objects underfoot.

  1. Spend two minutes on one paired target, letting the ball bounce and keeping the pace cooperative.
  2. Change the target pair for two minutes, then switch court halves and repeat both pairs.
  3. Count the longest rally and note balls that land in the target but sit high enough to attack.

Build it Call the next target before contact, or change placement after four neutral dinks. Keep control before adding pressure.

Success target One rally of 10 controlled dinks from each court half, with at least 6 reaching the chosen areas.

04Forehand: crosscourt to middleintermediate · 6 min · 2+

Set up Hitter near the baseline; feeder across the net supplies comfortable bounced forehands. Agree on crosscourt and middle target areas.

  1. Turn before the bounce and contact in front. Send five forehands crosscourt at a pace the feeder can handle.
  2. Send five toward the middle, then let the feeder call the target before the bounce for another 10.
  3. Swap roles after three minutes. Count placement, balance, and readiness for the next ball.

Build it Vary feed depth, then play the reply. For a right-side hitter, add recovery from the forehand sideline toward the agreed seam.

Success target 7 of 10 called targets reached in court while finishing balanced.

05The drop ladderintermediate · 8 min · 2+

Set up Feeder at the kitchen line; hitter starts a few steps behind their own kitchen line. Use the middle of the opposing kitchen as the target.

  1. On five comfortable bounced feeds, lift the ball softly into the target kitchen.
  2. After 3 of 5 successful drops, move the starting position two steps farther from the net; work toward the baseline.
  3. After a good drop, move forward and stop for the feeder's next contact. Swap roles halfway.

Build it Add a deeper feed or forehand/backhand variation. Return to the previous distance if touch disappears.

Success target 3 of 5 drops land in the kitchen and require the feeder to contact below net height at two different starting distances.

06Reset your way forwardintermediate · 8 min · 2+

Set up Feeder at the kitchen line; hitter in midcourt. Feed at controlled pace with enough spacing to reset between balls.

  1. Send five feeds toward the hitter's feet or comfortable reach. The hitter uses a short stroke to soften the reply into the kitchen.
  2. After a low reset, take one step forward; stop and prepare as the feeder contacts the next ball.
  3. If the reset sits up, hold position and handle the next feed. Swap roles after four minutes.

Build it Vary direction before increasing pace. Add a partner moving beside the hitter once individual control is consistent.

Success target 3 of 5 resets land softly in the kitchen; complete one three-ball forward sequence without a rushed contact.

07Right side + shared seamintermediate · 8 min · 3–4+

Set up A doubles pair near the kitchen line faces one or two cooperative feeders. Right and left mean the player's view facing the net. Agree who takes the seam, accounting for handedness.

  1. Feed five balls toward the right sideline. The right-side player handles the ball while the partner shifts to protect the middle.
  2. Feed five middle balls. Use an early 'mine' or 'yours' call and keep the other player available for the next shot.
  3. Mix the two locations for 10 feeds, then swap court-side roles. With four players, trade feeding and receiving roles halfway.

Build it Play two follow-up shots after each feed, then add a controlled pace change from the right-side player and cover its counter.

Success target 10 mixed feeds with one clear hitter per ball, no collision, and both players prepared for the reply.

08Attack or reset?advanced · 8 min · 2–4+

Set up Players at the kitchen lines. Start cooperatively on one court half with two players, or as doubles with four. Agree to controlled pace and the same decision goal.

  1. Begin each sequence with three cooperative dinks. The feeder then varies a low ball and a ball that allows a higher, balanced contact.
  2. Keep the low ball soft. On a suitable higher ball, choose a controlled speed-up and prepare for the counter; choosing another dink is allowed.
  3. Play two more shots, then discuss the decision. Swap the decision-making role after five sequences.

Build it Remove the predictable fourth-feed timing, then use normal points. Score decision quality separately from winning the rally.

Success target 8 of 10 choices suit the contact height and balance, with recovery for the reply and no kitchen fault.
